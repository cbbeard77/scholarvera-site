Formatting is only half the problem.

A conventional checker tells you whether a reference looks right. InForm goes further and checks whether the source behind it is actually there.

i.

APA 7 guidance

Font, spacing, headings, page numbers, block quotations, statistics, and the reference list. InForm names each issue, points to the APA 7 section behind it, and explains why it matters, so your next paper is better than your last.

ii.

Citation and reference matching

Every in-text citation should have a matching reference, and every reference should be cited. InForm reconciles the two and shows you what is missing on either side.

iii.

Source verification

InForm checks each cited work against several scholarly databases to confirm it is real, catches DOIs that do not resolve, and flags sources that have been retracted or corrected. This is the part a spell-checker cannot do.

Teach, don't fix

It shows you what to change. You make the call.

InForm never rewrites your sentences or changes your citations. Its review is non-destructive: nothing in your document moves unless you ask it to.

You can insert selected findings as Word comments, or build a clean, tick-to-assemble list of feedback to hand a student. Either way, the words on the page stay yours.

What InForm checks

A non-destructive review of your document, grouped the way you would review it yourself.

Formatting

  • APA 7 fonts and sizes
  • Double spacing and one-inch margins
  • Page numbers and running head
  • Heading levels and format
  • Paragraph and block-quote indents
  • Statistics and leading zeros
  • Tracked changes left in the draft

References

  • Hanging indent and size
  • Reference-list order
  • Reference list on a new page
  • In-text and reference-list agreement
  • Missing or uncited entries

Sources

  • Existence across scholarly databases
  • DOI resolution
  • Retractions and corrections
  • Live and archived web sources
  • Details that disagree with the record

Questions

Does InForm change my writing?

No. InForm never changes your writing. Its review is non-destructive: it marks what needs attention and explains why, and the only thing it ever adds to your document is a comment, and only when you choose. It never rewrites a sentence or auto-corrects a citation.

Does InForm send my paper, or its text, to an AI model?

No. The formatting checks run on your own machine, and source verification is done by looking references up in bibliographic databases, not by an AI model. Your prose is never sent anywhere. Only the reference entry itself leaves your machine, and only to confirm the work exists.

What does source verification actually do?

For each work you cite, InForm looks it up across several scholarly databases and resolvers to confirm the source exists, checks whether a cited DOI resolves, flags works that have been retracted or corrected, and notes details that disagree with the authoritative record. It reconciles more than one source before drawing a conclusion.

Which databases does InForm use to verify sources?

Depending on the kind of work, InForm consults Crossref, OpenAlex, ERIC, Google Books, and Open Library, resolves DOIs directly, checks retraction and correction records, and for web sources checks the live page and web archives. Books are checked against book catalogues, journal articles against article indexes.

What happens when InForm cannot verify a legitimate source?

It says so plainly. Some legitimate works are hard to confirm automatically, such as unusual books, gray literature, older material, or local documents. In those cases InForm reports that it could not verify the source rather than claiming it is fake. It treats "cannot verify" and "does not exist" as different things, so an inability to verify is not presented as proof a source is fake.

Where does it run?

Inside Microsoft Word, as an add-in, on Windows, Mac, and Word on the web. The formatting checks run on your own machine; only source verification reaches out, and only with the reference entry, never your prose.

Can faculty use InForm to review student papers?

Yes. InForm works on your own writing and also gives an instructor a review surface for reading a student's paper, where findings and source checks can be turned into comments or a feedback list. The judgment always stays with the reviewer.

Which citation styles are supported?

APA 7 today. Support for additional styles is planned, and beta participants help decide what comes next.
